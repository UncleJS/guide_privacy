Tools, settings, and laws change. These primary sources are maintained by their publishers and are worth rechecking before you act on any specific step in this guide. Links point to site home pages; search for the topic within each site.

Last reviewed: 2026-10-21.

General guides

  • Electronic Frontier Foundation: Surveillance Self-Defense. Guides on threat modeling, messaging, devices, and specific situations such as protests and border crossings.
  • Privacy Guides. Community-maintained recommendations for tools and services, with criteria for each category.
  • Mozilla: Privacy Not Included. Reviews of the privacy practices of connected products.

Accounts and breaches

  • Have I Been Pwned. Check whether an email address appears in known breaches, and subscribe to alerts.
  • FIDO Alliance: Passkeys. Background on passkeys and hardware security keys.

Messaging and tools

  • Signal Support. Setup, usernames, disappearing messages, and safety numbers.
  • Tor Project. Download and verification, bridges, and usage guidance.

Platform security documentation

  • Apple Platform Security. How Apple devices protect data, including backups and Advanced Data Protection.
  • Google Android security and the Google Account help center. Device and account security settings.
  • Microsoft Windows security documentation. Device encryption, BitLocker, and privacy controls.

Help for higher-risk situations

  • Access Now Digital Security Helpline. Free, confidential help for civil-society groups, journalists, and activists.
  • Coalition Against Stalkerware. Resources for people who suspect stalkerware, and for supporters.
  • Safety Net, National Network to End Domestic Violence. Technology safety planning for survivors and advocates.
  • Committee to Protect Journalists. Safety resources for journalists.

If you live outside the United States, look for the equivalent national or regional organizations for digital rights, domestic-abuse support, and legal aid.

Laws and rights

  • EU GDPR overview and your national data-protection authority, for access, deletion, and objection rights.
  • Your state or national consumer-privacy law (for example California's CCPA/CPRA), for opt-out and deletion requests.
  • A lawyer or digital-rights organization in your country, for border searches, device seizure, and evidence questions.

About these sources

This guide summarizes ideas in its own words. For a decision that matters, read the original source and check its date. Listing a source here is not an endorsement; see the Disclaimer and Limitation of Liability.
