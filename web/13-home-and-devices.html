13. Home and devices

On this page
  • Who this is for
  • What leaks
  • Steps
  • What this does not fix
  • Checklist

Who this is for

Anyone with a home network, smart speakers, cameras, a smart TV, or other connected devices.

What leaks

  • Microphones and cameras. Smart speakers, video doorbells, baby monitors, and TVs can record audio and video.
  • Viewing and usage data. Smart TVs report what you watch, and some listen to the room.
  • Device telemetry. Appliances, thermostats, and lights report usage patterns that reveal when you are home.
  • Weak security. Cheap devices ship with default passwords and rarely get updates.
  • Network exposure. One compromised device on your network can reach the others.
  • Neighbors and visitors. Cameras that cover shared spaces record people who did not agree.

Steps

1. Buy fewer connected devices

  • For each device ask what it does that an unconnected version would not, and whether you need it.
  • Prefer products that work locally, without an account or cloud service.
  • Check how long the vendor promises security updates before you buy.

2. Segment the network

  • Put smart-home devices, TVs, and guests on a separate guest or IoT network, away from your computers and phones.
  • Use a router that supports multiple networks, and keep its firmware current (chapter 06).
  • Turn off UPnP and remote administration on the router.

3. Secure each device

  • Change default passwords on first setup. Use a unique password from your password manager.
  • Install updates, and turn on automatic updates if offered.
  • Create the vendor account with a unique password and 2FA.
  • Turn off features you do not use: remote access, voice purchasing, always-listening modes.
  • Review what the companion app is allowed to access on your phone (chapter 09).

4. Smart speakers and voice assistants

  • Use the mute switch when you want certainty.
  • Delete stored voice recordings and turn off retention of voice history, if offered.
  • Disable voice shopping and linked services you do not need.
  • Keep speakers out of bedrooms and rooms where you discuss sensitive matters.

5. Smart TVs

  • Turn off automatic content recognition (ACR), viewing-data sharing, and personalized ads in the TV's settings. Menu names differ by manufacturer; look under privacy or terms.
  • Do not sign in to accounts you do not need.
  • Disconnect the TV from the internet and use a separate streaming device you control, if you prefer.

6. Cameras

  • Choose cameras that support local storage and end-to-end encryption, or review the provider's access policy.
  • Use 2FA on the camera account. Camera account takeovers are common and highly invasive.
  • Point cameras only at your property and not at neighbors' windows or yards. Many places have laws about recording others.
  • Tell guests and household members where cameras are.
  • Cover or unplug cameras in private spaces, or do not install them there.

7. Disposal

  • Factory reset and remove from your account before selling, recycling, or giving away a device.

What this does not fix

  • Vendors can change features and policies after you buy.
  • Segmenting does not stop a device from sending data to its maker.
  • A camera or microphone you control can still be misused by someone who gets into the account.
