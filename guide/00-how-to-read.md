# 00. How to read this guide

[Home](../README.md) | Next: [Privacy, security, and anonymity](01-privacy-security-anonymity.md)

## Who this is for

Anyone who wants to leak less about themselves online. You do not need technical training. The steps are ordered so the first ones give the largest benefit for the least effort.

## What this guide covers

- **Part 1 (chapters 03 to 16):** everyday practices for accounts, email, browsers, networks, messaging, social media, phones, computers, cloud storage, payments, home devices, households, data brokers, and account recovery.
- **Part 2 (chapters 17 to 23):** higher-risk situations where a specific person or organization is trying to learn about you. These chapters assume the Part 1 baseline.

## What this guide will not do

- It will not make you anonymous. Anonymity is a separate, harder goal ([chapter 01](01-privacy-security-anonymity.md)).
- It will not replace legal advice. Rules on searches, device access, encryption, and data retention vary by country and change over time.
- It will not rank every product. Product lists go stale; the guide names a default where one is well established and explains how to evaluate alternatives.
- It does not include methods for accessing other people's accounts or devices, tracking people, or evading a lawful investigation.

## How each chapter works

Every chapter has the same parts:

1. **Who this is for** and the threat it addresses
2. **What leaks**: the specific information that escapes, and to whom
3. **Steps**: one default choice, with a short note on alternatives
4. **What this does not fix**
5. **Checklist**

## How to use the checklists

Do not try to finish the whole guide in one sitting. A reasonable order:

1. Read chapters 01 and 02 and write down your threat model in a few sentences.
2. Do the checklists in chapters 03 (accounts) and 04 (email) first. Account compromise is the most common serious privacy failure.
3. Work through the rest of Part 1 in any order, starting with the devices you use most.
4. Read Part 2 only if chapter 02 puts you at a higher risk level.

## Keeping it current

Settings menus move and apps change. Where a step names a setting, search for the setting's name in the current vendor documentation if you cannot find it. The [further reading](further-reading.md) page lists primary sources worth rechecking.
