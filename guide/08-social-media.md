## Who this is for

Anyone with a social account, including accounts you rarely open. Social platforms hold some of the most detailed profiles of you, and they are a main source for doxxing and social-engineering attacks.

[↑ Back to TOC](#toc)

## What leaks

- **Posts, photos, and videos,** including metadata such as location, time, and the device used.
- **Your network.** Friends, followers, and group memberships reveal where you live, work, and study.
- **Behavior.** What you like, linger on, and share, even without posting.
- **Other people's posts about you.** Tags, photos, and mentions you did not control.
- **Linkage.** The same username, photo, or writing style across accounts connects a pseudonymous account to your real one.
- **Off-platform tracking.** Embedded buttons and tracking pixels report your browsing to the platform even when you are not on it.

## Steps

### 1. Choose which accounts to keep

- Delete accounts you do not use. Deactivating usually leaves the data in place; check that deletion is actually available and how long it takes.
- Download your data before deleting if you want a copy.

### 2. Lock down each account

- Set the account to private or friends-only if you do not need a public audience.
- Limit who can find you by phone number or email.
- Turn on review for tags and mentions before they appear on your profile.
- Restrict old posts to a narrower audience in one step, using the platform's bulk-change tool.
- Review third-party apps and "Sign in with" connections, and remove those you do not recognize.
- Turn off personalized ads and ad tracking from partners in the ad settings.
- Turn off location history and location tags on posts.

### 3. Separate audiences

- Use different accounts, or friends lists, for family, work, and public life.
- Decide in advance what you will never post: home address, children's school, travel plans in advance, documents, boarding passes, keys, vehicle plates.
- Remember that "close friends" lists work only as well as the friends on them.

### 4. Strip metadata from what you share

- Many platforms remove location data from photos on upload, but not all do and not always. Remove location data from images yourself before sharing in forums, email, or messaging.
- Check the background of photos: street signs, mail, school logos, reflections, and landmarks give away location.
- Avoid posting photos of other people without asking.

### 5. If you want a pseudonymous account

Pseudonyms reduce linkage but do not provide anonymity. To lower the risk:

- Use a distinct username, email address, photo, and bio not used elsewhere.
- Do not sign in to it from the same browser profile as your real accounts.
- Do not follow the same people, post at the same times, or write in the same distinctive style as your real account.
- Avoid mentioning real places, workplaces, or events that narrow your identity.
- See [chapter 18](18-compartments.md) for deeper separation.

### 6. Reduce what others can learn about you

- Search for yourself regularly, signed out, and see what shows up.
- Ask friends and family not to tag you in locations or post your children's identifying details.
- Use the platform's report tools for impersonation, doxxing, or non-consensual images, and keep records.

## What this does not fix

- Platforms keep data you delete from your profile for a time and may keep it in backups or for other purposes.
- Screenshots and archives mean anything you posted publicly may exist elsewhere.
- Private settings do not protect you from the platform itself.

## Checklist

- [ ] Unused accounts deleted.
- [ ] Remaining accounts set to the narrowest audience I need.
- [ ] Tag review, ad personalization, and location history set.
- [ ] Third-party app connections reviewed.
- [ ] I know what I will not post.
- [ ] I searched for my name signed out.
