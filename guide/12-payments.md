## Who this is for

Anyone who pays online or in shops. Payment records reveal where you live, what you buy, your health, your beliefs, and your routines.

[↑ Back to TOC](#toc)

## What leaks

- **Card networks and banks.** They see every transaction and sell insights from the data.
- **Merchants.** They keep your name, address, phone number, order history, and sometimes your card.
- **Loyalty programs.** They link your purchases across years and to your identity.
- **Payment apps.** Some show your transactions to friends by default.
- **Data brokers.** They buy and combine purchase data.
- **Shipping.** Your address ends up in many databases.

## Steps

### 1. Protect the payment accounts themselves

- Use a passkey or hardware key on your bank and payment accounts ([chapter 03](03-accounts.md)).
- Turn on transaction alerts and review statements monthly.
- Freeze or lock your card in the app when you are not using it, if your bank offers it.

### 2. Reduce what merchants keep

- Check out as a guest where possible instead of creating an account.
- Do not save your card with merchants you rarely use.
- Use virtual cards or masked card numbers where your bank or a payment service offers them. A virtual card limits damage from a breach and prevents a merchant from linking subscriptions to your main card.
- Set spending limits on virtual cards for trials and subscriptions.
- Use a wallet on your phone (tap to pay): the merchant gets a token instead of your card number.

### 3. Handle loyalty programs

- Decide whether the discount is worth the profile. For routine shopping at one store the trade may be worthwhile.
- Use an alias email and a separate phone number for loyalty sign-ups ([chapter 04](04-email.md)).
- Avoid linking loyalty accounts to your main email or card where you can.

### 4. Payment apps and social payments

- Set payment apps to private, so that transactions do not appear in public or friends feeds.
- Write neutral notes. Notes on payments are often visible to the app company.
- Verify the recipient before sending. Scammers rely on look-alike names.
- Do not send money to someone you have not verified, and treat requests for unusual payment methods (gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency) as scam signals.

### 5. Cash and alternatives

- Cash leaves no digital record at the point of sale. It is useful for sensitive local purchases.
- Prepaid cards bought with cash reduce linkage but may require identity verification and often carry fees.
- Cryptocurrency is generally not private: most blockchains are public ledgers, and exchanges collect identity documents. Treat it as pseudonymous at best.

### 6. Shipping and addresses

- Use a parcel locker, a work address, or a mail-forwarding service if you do not want a retailer to hold your home address.
- Remove saved addresses from accounts you no longer use.

### 7. If a card is exposed

- Contact your bank, freeze or replace the card, and dispute fraudulent charges quickly.
- Replace the card number at every service that stores it. A virtual card makes this easier.
- Place a fraud alert or credit freeze if your identity information was exposed ([chapter 15](15-data-brokers-and-breaches.md)).

## What this does not fix

- Banks and card networks must keep records, and they share them under law.
- Digital payments cannot be fully anonymous.
- A merchant that needs to ship to you will know your address.

[↑ Back to TOC](#toc)

## Checklist

- [ ] Strong sign-in on banking and payment apps; alerts on.
- [ ] Virtual or masked card numbers for online use where available.
- [ ] Guest checkout by default; fewer saved cards.
- [ ] Payment app transactions set to private.
- [ ] A plan for shipping to somewhere other than home when needed.
